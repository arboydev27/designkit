# Swiss design: DesignKit reference

Version 2.0.0. A contemporary interpretation of this direction. The three projects vary content, palette, and typography while preserving the style’s defining materials and composition. Treat suggested colors and typefaces as adaptable choices.

## Character

Swiss graphic design is associated with disciplined grids, asymmetric composition, and a clear typographic hierarchy. Its systematic approach lets typography, photography, and space communicate with precision.

Typographic order, disciplined alignment, asymmetry, and concise graphic contrast.

- Systematic grids
- Sans-serif type
- Asymmetric balance
- Clear hierarchy

## Three interpretations

### FORM / 25 (event)

Three sessions on typography, objects, and the systems that connect them.

The programme reads through scale and alignment rather than ornamental containers.

## Composition and behavior

Align text and imagery to a visible grid; communicate hierarchy through scale, weight, and position.

Prefer immediate state changes and restrained fades; avoid decorative bounce.

## Useful decisions

- Define the grid before placing content.
- Use alignment to connect distant elements.
- Create hierarchy with scale and weight.

## Common mistakes

- Confusing precision with perfect symmetry.
- Adding decoration without a role.
- Using too many type sizes.
