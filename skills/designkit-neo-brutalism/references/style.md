# Neo-brutalism: DesignKit reference

Version 2.0.0. A contemporary interpretation of this direction. The three projects vary content, palette, and typography while preserving the style’s defining materials and composition. Treat suggested colors and typefaces as adaptable choices.

## Character

Neo-brutalist interfaces combine blunt geometry, strong outlines, hard offset shadows, and conspicuous color. Their playful directness makes hierarchy visible and gives ordinary controls a graphic, poster-like presence.

Heavy outlines and hard shadows give ordinary controls poster-like presence.

- Heavy outlines
- Hard shadows
- Loud contrast
- Direct typography

## Three interpretations

### LOUD® (studio)

Identity and digital experiences for people with something impossible to ignore.

An oversized declaration and blunt project tiles convey the studio's direct personality.

### Elsewhere press (shop)

Big prints, small editions, and absolutely no polite beige rectangles.

Hard-edged product panels make the print, price, and add action easy to distinguish.

Preview: https://designkit-vert.vercel.app/examples/neo-brutalism-print-shop/
Reference: examples/neo-brutalism-print-shop.md

### GOOD NOISE (event)

Independent music, experimental talks, and a room full of people making something happen.

Large schedule labels and outlined ticket controls give useful information the same energy as the poster.

## Composition and behavior

Build a blunt graphic hierarchy with thick rules, one oversized headline, and hard-edged panels.

A pressed control moves into its offset shadow immediately; navigation stays direct.

## Useful decisions

- Choose one consistent outline weight.
- Use shadow direction to unify the interface.
- Reserve the strongest color for primary actions.

## Common mistakes

- Competing emphasis on every control.
- Turning body copy into all caps.
- Shadows that obscure adjacent content.

## Suitability

- Creative tools
- Independent brands
- Event campaigns

Choose a relevant interpretation or compose a new one for the actual project. Do not turn a visual style into a requirement to use a particular page structure.
