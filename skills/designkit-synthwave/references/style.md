# Synthwave: DesignKit reference

Version 2.0.0. A contemporary interpretation of this direction. The three projects vary content, palette, and typography while preserving the style’s defining materials and composition. Treat suggested colors and typefaces as adaptable choices.

## Character

Synthwave visuals draw on imagined 1980s futures, electronic music, and cinematic neon landscapes. Horizon grids, glowing suns, and purple-pink gradients create a nostalgic, night-driving atmosphere.

Retro-futurist horizons, luminous accent lines, and cinematic contrast.

- Neon horizons
- Perspective grids
- Sunset gradients
- Retro futurism

## Three interpretations

### NIGHTDRIVE (event)

An evening of electronic music, neon memories, and sounds from the future we once imagined.

A static horizon sets the mood behind a clearly structured event programme.

### tape deck (shop)

Independent electronic records and mixtapes for people who like the journey as much as the destination.

Cover-like panels use cinematic color while product descriptions retain high contrast.

Preview: https://designkit-vert.vercel.app/examples/synthwave-record-shop/

### AFTER HOURS (journal)

Conversations with electronic artists, notes on sound, and the stories behind a favorite track.

The reading column brings editorial clarity to a deliberately cinematic visual language.

## Composition and behavior

Set a horizon-like visual behind a clear foreground panel; use a limited pink-violet spectrum.

Static horizons preserve the mood; keep interaction feedback brief and avoid flashing neon.

## Useful decisions

- Use the horizon as a visual focal point.
- Apply glow to decoration rather than body copy.
- Let dark areas provide resting space.

## Common mistakes

- Neon text on bright gradients.
- Animating every light source.
- Overusing grid patterns behind content.
